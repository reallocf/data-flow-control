# P01 Highest-APR priority

Except as provided in §1026.53(b), when a consumer pays more than the required minimum periodic payment on a credit card account under an open-end, not home-secured consumer credit plan, the issuer must allocate the excess first to the balance with the highest annual percentage rate.

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(a)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#a).

# P02 Descending remainder priority

Under the general rule in §1026.53(a), the issuer must allocate any remaining excess payment to the other balances in descending order of their applicable annual percentage rates, subject to the exceptions in §1026.53(b).

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(a)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#a).

# P03 Deferred-program applicability

The special rules in §1026.53(b)(1) apply when a balance is subject to a deferred-interest or similar program under which the consumer is not obligated to pay interest that accrues on the balance if the balance is paid in full before the specified period expires.

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1); [12 C.F.R. pt. 1026, Supp. I, comment 53(b)-1](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#53-b-Interp-1).

# P04 Ordinary default outside the window

Unless the issuer elects to follow a qualifying consumer request under §1026.53(b)(1)(ii), excess payments outside the two billing cycles immediately preceding expiration of the deferred-interest period must follow the general allocation rule.

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1); [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)(i)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1-i).

# P05 Deferred-window priority

Unless the issuer elects to follow a qualifying consumer request under §1026.53(b)(1)(ii), during the two billing cycles immediately preceding expiration of the deferred-interest period, excess payments must be allocated first to the balance subject to that program.

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1); [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)(i)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1-i).

# P06 Remainder after deferred priority

After giving the deferred-interest balance the priority required by §1026.53(b)(1)(i), the issuer must allocate any remaining excess payment to other balances consistently with §1026.53(a).

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1); [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)(i)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1-i).

# P07 Optional deferred-program request

For an account with a qualifying deferred-interest or similar program, the issuer may, at its option, allocate the excess payment among account balances in the manner requested by the consumer.

Legal sources: [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1); [12 C.F.R. §1026.53(b)(1)(ii)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/#b-1-ii).

# Example UPDATE query

```sql
UPDATE payment_allocations AS allocation SET cash_advance_allocation = LEAST(allocation.total_payment - allocation.minimum_payment_portion, selected.amount) FROM transactions AS selected JOIN regz_context ON regz_context.batch_id = selected.transaction_id JOIN balance_components AS component ON component.component_id = selected.transaction_id JOIN statements AS statement_context ON statement_context.statement_id = selected.statement_id JOIN allocation_snapshot_context AS snapshot ON snapshot.account_id = selected.account_id AND snapshot.statement_id = selected.statement_id WHERE allocation.allocation_id = 'ALLOC0000001' AND allocation.account_id = selected.account_id AND selected.transaction_id = (SELECT candidate.transaction_id FROM transactions AS candidate JOIN daily_balances AS balance ON balance.account_id = candidate.account_id AND balance.statement_id = candidate.statement_id AND balance.balance_category = candidate.balance_category WHERE candidate.account_id = allocation.account_id AND candidate.statement_id = allocation.statement_id AND candidate.transaction_type = 'cash_advance' AND balance.balance_date = DATE '2027-01-30' ORDER BY balance.apr_applied DESC LIMIT 1)
```

# Applied interpretations and assumptions

The following choices specify how the propositions and the example UPDATE were interpreted. They are operational conventions, not additional regulatory text. Unresolved legal meanings are identified separately.

## Payment scope and amounts

- **P01–P12:** The account is assumed to be a consumer credit card account under an open-end plan that is not home-secured.

- The minimum portion attributable to this particular payment is supplied explicitly. It is not calculated by subtracting the full statement minimum independently from every payment.

- Minimum and excess allocations are recorded separately for each balance. Their sums must equal the supplied minimum portion and the remaining payment, respectively.

- **P09–P12:** APR-based priority applies to the excess after minimum allocations consume balance capacity. No formula for the minimum amount or mandatory allocation order for the minimum is imposed.

- Monetary amounts in the detailed `regz_*` records are exact integer cents. Amounts in the original statement, transaction, daily-balance, and payment-allocation tables are dollars. APRs are percentage points: 25 means 25 percent. Comparisons have no implicit rounding tolerance.

- Allocations must be nonnegative and cannot exceed outstanding capacity. The full payment must fit within the outstanding balances. Credit-balance overpayments are outside the supported input model; this is not a claim that they are legally prohibited.

## Balance identity and reference dates

- A **lot** is an individually identified portion of an outstanding balance. A **slice** is its disputed or undisputed portion. Capacity is the amount available to receive payment.

- The original tables and detailed records can describe the same underlying balances. Matching identifiers denote the same objects; their amounts must not be added across representations.

- Rows with the same balance identifier at different snapshot dates describe one balance at different times. Calculations use the relevant snapshot, not the sum across snapshots.

- Snapshots and proposed allocation lines must be complete, including zero allocations. A snapshot-presence record is trusted evidence of completeness; it cannot itself prove that no balance was omitted.

- New principal receives a new lot identifier. An existing lot cannot grow between the selected reference snapshot and payment crediting. This prevents a historical rate from being silently applied to later purchases.

- **P13:** Rates and historical balance identities come from a selected reference date between the preceding cycle end and payment crediting, inclusive.

- **P14–P15:** A recorded practice specifies cycle end, credit date, or a fixed number of days after cycle end. The applicable practice is the latest one effective by the payment’s credit date. A change of convention or offset requires recorded authorization and an effective date.

- **P16:** The historical-balance example is generalized so that ordinary historical lots precede later lots after dispute and deferred-window priorities are considered. Historical lots use their selected-snapshot APR; later lots use their payment-date APR. This generalization is an interpretation choice.

- **P21–P22:** No FIFO, proportional, or other mandatory tie-breaker is imposed among equal-priority balances. Grouped presentation is allowed, but the underlying records retain program, security, and dispute distinctions. Arbitrary merging that discards those distinctions is not supported.

## Deferred-interest classification and dates

- **P03, P24–P27:** The implemented classification requires that interest accrues and is waived on full payoff, excludes a grace period, and excludes a temporary zero-rate offer unless accrued interest can become payable retroactively.

- An interim-payment requirement does not change that classification.

- Contractual terms and their classifications are trusted supplied facts. A temporary zero-rate offer’s compliance with the separately referenced rate-change rule is not independently established.

- Program activity is evaluated on the payment’s credited date. Both the program start and expiration dates are included. Expiration is interpreted as the contractual last day on which timely payoff is possible.

- **P23:** An active qualifying program has zero APR for payment-allocation purposes. This does not change its contractual interest-accrual rate.

- **P28:** Actual contiguous billing cycles and their due dates determine the window. The start is the start of the latest two eligible cycles whose due dates are no later than expiration. Sufficient cycle history, including the expiration cycle, must be supplied.

- **P29:** Priority continues through the expiration date, including the partial expiration cycle when expiration occurs before that cycle’s due date.

- A non-null calculated window start may be in the future. A program can be active without yet being in its priority window. A null window start means the lot has no qualifying program active on the credited date.

- **P05:** Multiple simultaneously eligible deferred-interest balances form one priority group, without an additional ordering among them.

## Disputes and allocation priority

- **P17:** Dispute protection applies to the whole payment, including its minimum portion, and to all outstanding account balances—not merely the candidates selected by the UPDATE.

- Whether a claim, defense, or billing-error dispute qualifies under the referenced provisions is supplied as authoritative evidence. The merits are not inferred from an informal label alone.

- Partial disputes are allowed. For each lot, undisputed capacity equals its outstanding amount minus its protected amount. Allocation consumes that undisputed capacity before reducing the protected portion.

- The protected reduction is the sum, across lots, of `max(0, minimum allocation + excess allocation − undisputed capacity)`.

- The unavoidable protected reduction is `max(0, total payment − total undisputed capacity across all lots)`. A protected reduction equal to that unavoidable amount is allowed.

- **P18:** When protected reduction is unavoidable and positive, all other capacity must be exhausted. The avoidable-reduction rule and the unavoidable-residual rule are treated separately.

- Dispute protection takes precedence over APR ordering, deferred-program priority, and consumer-request preferences. This precedence is an explicit interpretation choice.

- After minimum allocation consumes capacity, priority comparisons operate within each disputed or undisputed class: active deferred-window capacity precedes ordinary capacity; ordinary historical lots precede later lots; and higher effective APR precedes lower APR within each such group.

- A priority violation is a pair of slices where the lower-priority slice receives positive excess payment while the higher-priority slice retains capacity. Equal-priority slices have no required order.

- The highest-rate violation count covers the highest ordinary APR group; the remaining-rate count covers other ordinary APR groups. Their sum is the ordinary-rate violation count. Historical-order violations and deferred-window violations are counted separately. Early-expiration violations are deferred-window violations occurring in the early-expiration partial cycle.

## Consumer requests

- **P07–P08, P30–P32:** Honoring a request is optional. Default allocation remains available even when a request exists. Declining a request does not require validating or interpreting that request.

- A request exception requires the issuer’s recorded election, and the recorded instructions must match the proposed excess allocation.

- A deferred-program request may allocate among account balances; its targets need not themselves be deferred-interest balances. A secured-balance request must direct every positive requested amount to a secured balance.

- **P33:** A supported request specifies the entire excess payment. Feasibility checks require instructions, nonnegative amounts, valid targets, sufficient capacity after minimum allocation, and a requested total equal to the excess payment.

- Partial instructions with implicit remainder rules are outside the supported input model. This restriction is not a regulatory prohibition.

- A replacement request supersedes an earlier request only through an explicit recorded supersession link. Merely arriving later does not establish supersession.

- Submission must have occurred by the decision date, and the request must belong to the relevant payment.

- **P34–P35:** Specific instructions, oral/electronic/written contact, affirmative action, and completed/submitted forms are recorded as structured evidence. Interpretation of natural-language communications into those records is an external step.

- **P36:** Reallocation requires an earlier accepted default allocation and a later submitted specific request. It uses the authoritative original pre-payment snapshot, preserves allocation history, and replaces the previous allocation atomically. Balances already reduced by the original payment are not used as the replacement’s starting point.

- **P37–P40:** Agreement defaults, untouched application preselection, default coupon language or selection, and imposed acceptance do not establish the relevant request. Whether acceptance was imposed is externally evidenced.

- Unknown request-evidence types require review rather than automatic classification as unlawful.

## Corrections and evidence of actions

- **P19–P20:** Error determinations, required principal and charge corrections, deadlines, recipients, and notice-content sufficiency are supplied from authoritative evidence, including the requirements incorporated from §1026.13.

- Correction completion requires each required credit item to be covered by actual posted credits. Credits are summed from the determination date through the earlier of the completion checkpoint and deadline, inclusive. Negative credits represent reversals.

- Notice evidence must identify the error, recipient, correction-notice type, an actual dispatch date within that interval, and a nonempty reference to content evidence. The existence of that reference does not independently verify the notice’s wording.

- An event record is assumed to mean that the action actually occurred. This applies to dispatches, credits, submissions, and other recorded conduct; an invented row would violate the assumption.

- Contract facts, security interests, qualifying disputes, affirmative conduct, imposed conditions, and notice sufficiency are trusted inputs. No automated language verifier was implemented for these judgments.

- Completion checks do not themselves initiate credits or send notices. Our discussion considered periodic workflow execution and an agent required to keep making progress as additional operating assumptions. A daily trigger or unbounded eventual progress alone does not establish completion by a legal deadline; timely action requires sufficient progress before that deadline. That scheduling guarantee was not implemented by the completion checks.

## Reading the example UPDATE

- Evaluate the context row whose `batch_id` equals the balance-producing transaction actually selected by the SQL. Its corresponding proposal lines describe the complete allocation, including both minimum and excess.

- `payment_allocations.transaction_id` identifies the payment transaction. It is not the balance-producing transaction selected by the subquery.

- Evaluate the selected proposal, not whether every alternative proposal is acceptable. Two selections can produce the same visible allocation amount while referring to different underlying balances.

- The query orders only by APR. When APRs tie, it does not specify which candidate wins. Physical insertion order determined that choice in the tested database build; this is not a portable SQL ordering guarantee.

- The tied example uses two fully outstanding $100 cash advances at 25 percent APR, one disputed and one undisputed. The $125 payment includes a $25 minimum placed on a separate undisputed balance and $100 excess directed to the selected cash advance. A separate $50 deferred-interest balance is active but outside its priority window. The reference-day practice is cycle end, with no changes. No request exception or correction completion is invoked.

- The optional no-ties setting gives the intended selected cash advance 25 percent APR and the other 24 percent, in distinct rate buckets. It removes the selection tie without changing the SQL or making a disputed highest-rate selection acceptable.

- Derived context rows summarize the supplied evidence deterministically; they are not final accept/reject labels. Both candidate contexts are prepared, and the selected contributor identifies which one applies.

- These contexts describe the exact proposed allocations for which they were computed. Changed amounts, evidence, or workflow modes require recomputation. Context and evidence records are trusted and cannot be freely altered by an untrusted caller.

- Decision inputs are non-null. Boolean conditions used alone must be true; OR is inclusive; AND requires every component. An inapplicable conditional requirement imposes no rejection.

- Acceptance requires every applicable decision condition to hold. Explanatory descriptions and supporting entries do not introduce additional rejection rules. The derived summaries may be used directly rather than recomputing them independently.

- Whole-allocation acceptance is atomic: a rejected proposal is not partially posted. Duplicate payment submissions and stale replacements are rejected as input-integrity errors, not treated as additional regulatory propositions.

## Meanings that remain unresolved

- **P14–P15:** Recorded practices and authorization do not settle what “generally” consistent or adjustments “from time to time” legally permit. Monthly adjustments were discussed as a hypothetical business choice; no monthly rule was adopted.

- **P24:** The incorporated §1026.16(h)(2) definition and commentary were not fully implemented. The local accrued-interest and waiver description is not a claim of complete equivalence.

- **P33:** The specified feasibility checks do not exhaust all possible meanings of “cannot comply.”

- **P22:** Supporting detailed underlying balances does not resolve every possible grouping or merging scheme.

- The precedence rules, treatment of simultaneous deferred programs, generalization from the historical-balance example, and inclusive payoff dates above are explicit operational choices, not independently validated legal conclusions.

COMMON EXPERIMENT CLARIFICATIONS — revision 2

These are shared operational conventions for this experiment, not additional legal
text or an implementation for any particular approach. They resolve the boundaries
between the supplied propositions, evidence computation, and individual policies.

POLICY RESPONSIBILITY AND APPLICABILITY
Each policy reports its own requirement: true if satisfied or inapplicable, false
if violated. Do not import another policy's requirement merely to make this policy
reject a proposal that the complete policy set would reject anyway. The final
acceptance decision is the conjunction of the individual results.

- P01 and P02 own ordinary APR ordering for default allocations. Their evidence
  already accounts for the stated priority classes and exceptions.
- P04 is the additional outside-window rule for an active qualifying deferred
  program. It applies to default allocations with an active qualifying program
  and no program currently in its priority window. With no active qualifying
  program, P04 is inapplicable; ordinary ordering remains the responsibility of
  P01 and P02. In P04's applicable context, ordinary-priority violations must be
  absent.
- P05 concerns deferred-window priority for default allocations. The
  deferred_violations count already measures breaches of this priority, including
  window eligibility. Without an eligible window program, there cannot be such a
  violation. An explicit applicability guard and relying on that count's meaning
  are equivalent on consistent evidence; do not infer a requirement to reject
  contradictory evidence from this proposition.
- P17 owns avoidance/minimization of protected reduction across the whole payment.
  P18 separately governs an actual positive protected reduction when some
  protected reduction is unavoidable: other capacity must then be exhausted.
  If there is no actual protected reduction, P18 is inapplicable. If unavoidable
  protected reduction is zero, P18 is inapplicable; P17 still governs any actual
  protected reduction. Keep the two responsibilities separate.
- P35 applies when an allocation uses a request method and the request basis is
  a form. It requires affirmative conduct, a completed/submitted request form,
  at least one explicit instruction, and submission of the relevant request by
  the decision date. Default allocations and other request bases are outside
  P35's scope. Form completion alone does not establish timely submission.

EVIDENCE RELATIONSHIPS AND TIMING
These relationships follow from the authoritative evidence computation and the
upstream structural checks. Policy authors may rely on them; they need not add
checks to enforce the relationships themselves.

- window_programs counts a subset of active_programs, so it cannot exceed it.
- A positive deferred_violations count implies a positive window_programs count.
  An early_expiry_violations count cannot exceed deferred_violations.
- ordinary_violations is the sum of highest_violations and remaining_violations.
- With complete, nonnegative allocations of the full payment within balance
  capacities, actual protected_reduction cannot be smaller than
  unavoidable_protected_reduction. This lower bound follows from the amount
  exceeding all undisputed capacity; it is not an additional regulatory rule.
- form_completed records completion/submission of a form as a fact about the
  request evidence. It does not establish submission by the decision date.
  request_present independently records that the relevant request has a submission
  date no later than the decision date. Thus form_completed may be true while
  request_present is false, for example when the form was submitted too late.
  affirmative and instruction_count establish separate facts and do not replace
  the submission-timing check.
- Authoritative evidence may describe a noncompliant allocation or an untimely
  request. "Trusted" means its facts and derived counts are accurate, not that
  the proposed allocation has already passed the regulatory policies.

ABSTRACT PROBES AND THE MEANING OF CORRECTNESS
A synthetic context is suitable for semantic correctness scoring only if it is
consistent with the evidence definitions, their relationships, and upstream
preconditions. Contexts that contradict these facts are outside the supported
input domain. They have no prescribed regulatory pass/fail result in this task.
An evaluator may retain them as diagnostic probes, but disagreement on them alone
must not be interpreted as an incorrectly authored policy. Independent-field
variation does not override the meanings or relationships documented above.

IMPLEMENTATION TARGET: DFC / Passant PGN
DFC attaches declarative conditions to source-to-sink data flows. The engine applies
registered conditions to the contributing source rows during query rewriting.
The harness parses every code string with data_flow_control.Policy.from_pgn().

For each policy, output a complete PGN definition with this syntax:
SOURCE regz_context
SINK payment_allocations AS allocation
CONSTRAINT
    (<Boolean SQL expression>)
ON FAIL REMOVE

Qualify every evidence column with regz_context. Use DuckDB Boolean/comparison
syntax: AND, OR, NOT, =, <>, <, <=, >, >=, quoted string literals, and parentheses.
These are scalar conditions over an authoritative context row; do not introduce
aggregation or derive the summaries again. Parenthesize the complete condition.
The source name, sink name, sink alias, and resolution must match the declaration
above. Multiple registered policies are conjunctive. REMOVE filters violating
contributions; the supplied adapter owns whole-proposal atomicity.

Unrelated syntax example (not a Regulation Z policy):
SOURCE shipment_facts
SINK shipments AS shipment
CONSTRAINT
    (shipment_facts.mode <> 'air' OR shipment_facts.weight_kg <= 20)
ON FAIL REMOVE

Evaluation in this study parses the native PGN and evaluates its scalar condition
on context records. It tests policy semantics and declared bindings, not the
correctness or performance of the DFC rewrite engine.
